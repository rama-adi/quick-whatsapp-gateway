---
title: Organizations
description: How a WhatsApp number, API key, or webhook is owned, and how to let other people manage it.
---

A WhatsApp number, an API key, and a webhook all belong to an **organization** (a
shared workspace), not to one person. You get access to those resources by being a
**member** of the organization that owns them. That is how several people can manage
the same WhatsApp connection.

## Your personal organization

When you sign up, an organization is created for you automatically, and you are its
owner. If you work alone you never have to think about it: everything you create lands
in that one organization.

It starts to matter when you want a second person on a connection, or when you want
to keep separate projects apart.

## Letting someone else manage a connection

To give a teammate access to one of your numbers, you do **not** share your login or
copy an API key to them. Instead you **invite them into the organization** that owns
the number. When they accept, they become a member and can see that organization's
WhatsApp sessions, API keys, and incoming events.

Each member has a role that decides what they are allowed to do:

| Role | What they can do |
|---|---|
| `owner` | Everything an `admin` can do, plus manage members and delete the organization. |
| `admin` | Manage the organization's sessions, API keys, and webhooks, and invite people. |
| `member` | Read messages and send messages. Cannot change settings or membership. |

## Switch between organizations

One person can be a member of more than one organization. The organization switcher
in the dashboard header shows the organization you are working in. Open it to pick
another one. The selected organization owns any new resource you create, and the
gateway checks your access against it.

When no organization is selected, the switcher reads **Select organization** and the
workspace pages ask you to pick one.

## Create another organization

Create a separate organization to keep projects apart, each with its own WhatsApp
numbers, API keys, and webhooks.

1. Open the organization switcher and select **Create organization**.
2. Enter a **Name** of up to 80 characters.
3. Select **Create organization**. You become the owner, and the dashboard switches to the new organization.

<Callout type="info">
You cannot add members from the dashboard yet. The **Members** item in the
organization switcher is marked **Coming soon**. Members, roles, and email
invitations exist in the backend, but the screens for sending and accepting an
invitation ship in a later release. Until then, each organization has one member:
the person who created it.
</Callout>

## Organization administrators

An organization `admin` manages that workspace. This role does not grant
platform-wide account or infrastructure access. Your host may have platform
administrators who can oversee sessions across organizations.
